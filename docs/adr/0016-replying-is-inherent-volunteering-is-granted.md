# Replying is inherent; volunteering is granted per Thread

There is one Resident definition, configured once and running in every Thread. So
nothing that distinguishes a family group from a dev channel can live in a prompt —
it arrives through Knowledge, through Briefs, or not at all. On the day the Harness
joins a room it has its personality, its Mandate, its tools and its reason for
being there, but nothing yet about the people in it or what is welcome.

Speaking therefore splits in two, because the two acts carry different social
contracts:

- **Replying when addressed is inherent.** Someone spoke to it; answering needs no
  permission. It can be switched off for a Thread, but it is on unless it is.
- **Volunteering is a Grant**, scoped to a Thread like every other. Speaking into a
  conversation nobody directed at it is the Harness asserting that its opinion is
  worth an interruption, and that is a claim someone should make deliberately.

## Why a Grant rather than a mode

**It is the mechanism we already have.** Grants scope by Thread and already answer
"what may this agent do here". Voice becomes one more line on a screen that exists,
revocable in one action, and no new concept is introduced.

**It fails in the right direction.** A Resident that is too quiet is disappointing
and you fix it by granting voice. A Resident that is too talkative in a real group
of colleagues gets muted or removed, and there is no second first impression. The
failure modes are not equally recoverable, so the default should not be symmetric.

**It is per-room, so correction is local.** Getting it wrong in the family chat is
fixed by revoking one Grant, not by retuning a global personality and risking every
other room in the process.

**It makes graduated autonomy concrete.** Voice is the first thing the Harness can
actually be _given_, which turns "it can earn more autonomy over time" from a
sentiment into an action with a screen and an audit trail.

**And silence stays legible, which is what makes the whole thing tunable.** A
Resident without voice still runs its Invocation, still considers whether it has
anything worth saying, and still closes with a Private Thought. So a week in a room
where it never spoke is not a blank — it is a record of every moment it considered
speaking and why it held back. Voice can then be granted on evidence rather than
hope, and revoked the same way.

## Consequences

- Two switches per Thread, not one: whether it answers when addressed, and whether
  it may speak unbidden. Most rooms will want the first and not the second.
- What makes it a good contributor in a given room is a fact about that room, held
  in Knowledge, and it accrues by being present — which it can do without voice.
- Reviewing unspoken thoughts is a real workflow and Rooms must support it, since
  it is how anyone decides whether to grant voice at all.
